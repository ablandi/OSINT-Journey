On September 15, I started an internship with *The OSINT Insider*, working under Rachel Brooks, the publication's Managing Editor.

It started the way most good things do — I reached out. I wrote to Rachel back on July 31 about learning from the team, she confirmed on August 19, and we got orientation and the syllabus sorted in early September. Day-to-day runs through Signal and Inspo, not email, which I respect.

The core of the internship is a course called **"Toggle Skills: Choosing Between OSINT and Traditional Journalism in Your Reporting."** It doubles as my compensation and as the required coursework for potentially onboarding to her team. The modules I'm getting the most out of so far are the OSINT and geolocation lessons tied to disaster reporting — the coursework uses areas hit by Hurricane Helene in October 2024 as case studies.

What I'm doing alongside it:

- Studying the back issues and the publication's promotion formula ahead of Issue #12.
- Reading — including a 2024 paper by Charlton, Mayer & Ohme on how OSINT communities and newsrooms divide the labor of verification. It maps uncomfortably well onto the exact question the Toggle Skills course is asking.
- Logging daily OSINT training reps — twenty focused minutes — in my own tracker.

Rachel also handed me my first mock project, which I've oriented on and parked while I work through the coursework.

Why this matters to me: it's the same thread as everything else here — Trace Labs, the Skull Games course, the applications I've been putting in. OSINT in service of something real.
