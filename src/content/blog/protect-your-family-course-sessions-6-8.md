On September 17, I finished the last three sessions of **Protect Your Family**, the counter-trafficking course from the [Skull Games Task Force](https://skullgames.io/) — two days ahead of the schedule I'd set for myself. I wrote up [sessions 1–5](/blog/protect-your-family-skull-games-course) earlier. This is the rest of the course.

## Session 6 — The Psychology of Exploitation

This session answers the question sitting underneath everything else in the course: *why does any of this work on a human mind?*

The frame is Maslow's hierarchy — physiological needs, safety, love and belonging, esteem, self-actualization — set alongside the Power & Control wheel, the catalog of domination tactics (intimidation, isolation, emotional abuse, coercion, and the rest) that show up wherever one person is being systematically controlled by another.

**My takeaway:** exploiters don't lead with force. They lead by finding the unmet need at one of those five levels and filling it on purpose — safety for the kid who doesn't feel safe at home, belonging for the one who doesn't feel it anywhere else. The need is real. The person filling it is the trap. Once you see that clearly, "why didn't they just leave" stops being a question you can ask with a straight face.

## Session 7 — The Telltale Signs

This is the practical session: five observable indicators of trafficking to watch for in the real world, plus two more terms for the vocabulary — "choose-up fee" and "branding."

**My takeaway:** indicators are only useful if you know what they're for, and what they're for is recognition, not intervention. My lane is the same one I set in session 2: observe carefully, and leave determination and action to law enforcement. The session made the signs concrete enough to actually spot, which is the entire point — you can't report what you can't see, and you can't protect your family from what you don't recognize.

## Session 8 — There is hope

The course closes the way it has to: with hope, and with concrete steps for what to do with everything you've just learned.

**My takeaway:** hope here isn't a feeling, it's an assignment. Some of the activation ground was already familiar — back in session 5 I'd started researching South Carolina's trafficking statutes and setting up a way to track legislation as it moves, so I can contact policymakers while bills are actually being decided. The course ends by pointing that kind of energy outward: learn the signs, teach them, and do something with them.

## The course, complete

Eight sessions, finished September 17 — two days early. I posted the completion to LinkedIn the same day.

What the course did, start to finish, is what I said I wanted after session 5: it replaced vague conviction with specific, usable understanding. The definitions. The cycle. The making of a trafficker. The demand side. The psychology. The signs. And finally, what to do about all of it.

I came in wanting to help. I came out with a clearer picture of what helping actually requires.
