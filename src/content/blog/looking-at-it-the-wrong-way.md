I've been working my Skull Games practice case, and I was stuck.

Not "I need another hour" stuck — properly stuck. I had a person of interest I was trying to run down, and no matter how I came at it, the trail wouldn't build. I was looking for the trafficker head-on, and head-on wasn't working.

So I did the thing I'm learning to do sooner instead of later: I asked for help. I hopped into a Slack huddle with one of the senior OSINT analysts, walked him through where I was, and he showed me I was looking at the whole problem the wrong way.

Here's what I learned.

**Victims often share the same number as their trafficker.** That was the reframe. I had a phone number, and I'd been treating it as one person's number. Run it through a people-search lookup, and sure enough — it came back with two names attached: the victim's and the male's. One number, both ends of the connection.

**Pivot to the victim.** Instead of hunting the POI directly, work the person next to him. I took the victim's first and middle name and Google dorked it scoped to Facebook — and there was her page. The trafficker keeps a low profile. The victim often doesn't, or can't.

**Victims signal loyalty.** Sometimes victims show their loyalty to their pimp right out in the open, shouting him out on social media. So on her page, I searched one word: "Daddy." And there it was — the connection to the POI, in her own words, on her own page.

Three lessons I'm keeping:

1. **Work the circle, not just the center.** The person you can't find is frequently standing next to someone you can.
2. **A shared identifier is a bridge.** One phone number connected two people I had been treating as separate problems.
3. **Ask early.** The huddle took minutes. My solo grinding had taken hours. This community teaches if you let it — that's not a weakness in the work, that *is* the work.

I'm still in training. This was a practice case, not a live one, and I won't pretend otherwise. But this is how the reps are supposed to go: get stuck, get taught, get sharper.

The watchman doesn't just watch. He learns how to look.
