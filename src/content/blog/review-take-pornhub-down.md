*Take Pornhub Down* is the most directly “OSINT-adjacent” book on this list. Laila Mickelwait, founder of the #Traffickinghub movement, writes this as a firsthand account of building an investigation and advocacy campaign against one of the largest tube sites in the world — and it reads with the pacing of a real-time case file.

What I found most compelling is how methodical the campaign was. This wasn’t outrage without follow-through; it was documentation, verification of specific uploaded videos depicting minors and non-consensual content, coordination with survivors and their legal teams, and sustained public pressure that eventually forced platform-level changes. As someone interested in OSINT skills specifically, this book is close to a case study in how public-facing investigative work can produce real accountability.

Mickelwait doesn’t shy away from the uncomfortable core issue: these platforms profited from unverified uploads for years, and it took relentless external pressure — not internal ethics — to force verification policies and content removal. That lines up directly with “The World” segment of *Brain, Heart, World*, but with far more operational detail about how the fight against it actually looked.

Reading this gave me a concrete sense of what “doing something” can look like beyond raising awareness — organizing evidence, working with survivors ethically, and applying sustained pressure on the platforms that enable exploitation at scale.

**Takeaway:** The clearest picture on this list of what a real, structured campaign against trafficking-enabling platforms looks like — essential reading if you’re drawn to the investigative side of this fight.
