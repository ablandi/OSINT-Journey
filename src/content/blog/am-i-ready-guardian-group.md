After I attended [ATII’s training](/blog/atii-follow-osint-data-review), my chest was poking out. I thought I was ready to run with some of my anti-human trafficking heroes — Matthew Parker from The Exodus Road, Tim Ballard before the scandal, and Jeff Tiegs from Skull Games. One day of training under my belt and I was already picturing myself alongside them.

I need to be clear about something, though. My heart is to serve the men, women, and children caught in the snares of human trafficking. I’m not trying to be a hero, and I don’t want to be the hero of their story. If anything, they are the heroes to me — the ones who survived, who are surviving, who fight every day just to hold onto themselves. But to serve them well, I needed good examples to follow, and I found those examples in the men I mentioned. Looking up to them was never about wanting the spotlight. It was about wanting to learn how to actually help.

## The humbling

After the ATII training, I thought I was fully equipped to start aiding in the fight. So I registered with Guardian Group.

I got humbled real quick. I couldn’t pass their practical exams. Man, my chest deflated fast.

I sat with that for a while. All that excitement, all that “I’m ready,” met head-on with the reality of how much I still didn’t know. It didn’t feel good in the moment, but looking back, it was exactly what I needed. Pride doesn’t survive contact with a real practical exam built by people who actually do this work.

That failure is what sent me back to the fundamentals — more reps through [KaseScenarios](/blog/kasescenarios-sinister-obsession-review), and enrolling in [Justen Charters’ OSINT Fundamentals course](/blog/osint-fundamentals-uncharted-review). Not to chase a badge this time, but because I finally understood how much I didn’t understand.

## Where I am now

Now here I am, after all that training, staring down the Guardian Group practical exams again. This time with more reverence and respect for the craft, and for the weight that comes with it. I’m not walking in with my chest out. I’m walking in humbled, aware of how much this work matters and how little room there is for ego in it.

And only one thought is going through my mind:

**Am I ready?**
