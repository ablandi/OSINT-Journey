Before I even knew what OSINT was — before I could put a name to what I actually wanted to do to help fight human trafficking — I came across an organization on LinkedIn called the **Anti-Human Trafficking Intelligence Initiative (ATII)**. At the time they were hosting a live training called *Follow OSINT Data – Fight Slavery Training*, on Wednesday, March 13, 2024, from 9am to 5pm EST. I signed up, and it ended up being the first time I saw the thing I’d been reaching for given a name, a structure, and a community.

## The agenda

The full day was packed, and it moved fast:

- **9:00am** — Intro to Training — Chris Kemp, ATII
- **9:30am** — Fighting Child Exploitation with Non-technical OSINT Methods — Julie Jones, Human-i Intelligence Services; Matt Richardson, ATII
- **11:00am** — 10 min Break
- **11:10am** — Fighting Human Trafficking (Sex and Labor) Through Targeted OSINT Data & Typologies — Silvija Krupena, Red Compass Labs; Chris Bagnell, Quantexa; and Chris Kemp, ATII
- **12:50pm** — 10 min Break
- **1:00pm** — Use Intelligence Processes Focused on OSINT to Empower Insights and Training to Counter Human Trafficking — Melissa Maranville, DeVille & Associates
- **2:30pm** — Leveraging OSINT in Anonymous Darknet & Blockchain Intelligence — Keven Hendricks, Ubivis Project and Michael Falls, ATII
- **4:00pm** — 10 min Break
- **4:10pm** — Conclusion, Test, and Badge Distribution

## What stuck with me

**Fighting Child Exploitation with Non-technical OSINT Methods** was the session that made everything click. The emphasis on *non-technical* mattered — it showed me you don’t need to be a hacker or a programmer to do meaningful research. Being methodical, patient, and careful with public information is a skill anyone committed enough can build.

**Targeted OSINT Data & Typologies** was my first real exposure to how financial institutions and data companies look at trafficking — not just as individual cases, but as patterns (typologies) you can systematically hunt for. That reframing — from “look for bad guys” to “look for the signatures exploitation leaves behind” — changed how I think about research entirely.

**Intelligence Processes Focused on OSINT** introduced me to the discipline side: collection plans, structured analysis, and how raw information becomes actual intelligence. This was the first time I’d heard the intelligence cycle explained by someone who lives it, and it made me realize how much rigor real investigators bring to work I’d been doing casually.

**Darknet & Blockchain Intelligence** was the most eye-opening technically. I went in knowing essentially nothing about darknet markets or tracing cryptocurrency, and came out understanding both the why and the how at a foundational level — enough to know what I wanted to learn next.

## Why this training mattered

Looking back, this was the day my journey got real. I had the motivation — that part had been burning for years — but this was the first time I saw a room (even a virtual one) full of professionals applying open source research directly against the exact problem I cared about. It told me two things: the skills I wanted to build actually existed as a discipline, and there were people already using them for good. All I had to do was catch up.

The test and badge at the end might sound like a small thing, but it was my first formal credential in this world — proof to myself that I could sit through a full day of this material and come out the other side understanding it.

This training is also what pointed me toward going deeper with structured learning, which eventually led me to the [Uncharted OSINT Fundamentals course](/blog/osint-fundamentals-uncharted-review) a couple of months later — and everything that’s come since.
