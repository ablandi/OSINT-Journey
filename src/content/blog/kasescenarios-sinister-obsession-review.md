Between taking [ATII’s live OSINT training](/blog/atii-follow-osint-data-review) in March 2024 and [Justen Charters’ OSINT Fundamentals course](/blog/osint-fundamentals-uncharted-review) in May 2024, I needed a way to keep the momentum going. Watching great instructors work is valuable, but skills only stick when you use them yourself. That’s when I started working through the OSINT challenges from [KaseScenarios](https://www.kasescenarios.com/).

KaseScenarios offers browser-based, scenario-driven challenges: you’re dropped into a realistic investigative situation and have to work through it using actual open source techniques — no hand-holding, no answer key until you’ve genuinely earned the solution. It was exactly the practice environment I needed at that point in my journey. And of all the scenarios I worked through, one stood out clearly as my favorite: **Sinister Obsession**.

## Why Sinister Obsession

Without spoiling anything for anyone who wants to try it themselves: the scenario drops you into a case centered on an anonymous online adversary — the kind of person who is confident they can’t be found. Your job is to prove them wrong, one data point at a time. The stakes of the fictional scenario feel real in a way that makes the work genuinely gripping, and the investigation builds on itself beautifully. Here’s what it taught me:

**Username enumeration and social media intelligence.** Sinister Obsession pushed me to treat a single username not as a dead string of characters, but as a trailhead. Tracking where that alias appeared across platforms, and reading what those different footprints revealed about the person behind it, turned SOCMINT from a concept I’d heard about into a skill I’d actually exercised.

**Geolocation: visually extracting data from an image.** One of the most satisfying parts of the scenario was answering contextual questions purely from visual evidence — reading an image the way an investigator reads it: what do the surroundings, the signage, the environment tell you about *where* and *when*? It trained me to slow down and actually look, instead of immediately reaching for a tool.

**Pivoting on key data points to uncover the identity behind an alias.** This was the through-line of the whole scenario, and honestly the core of OSINT itself. One small detail leads to an account, that account leads to another data point, and that pivot leads to the next. Piece by piece, the anonymous stops being anonymous. Working through that chain myself — rather than watching someone else do it — is what made the methodology finally feel like mine.

## Why practice scenarios matter

Looking back, this period of my journey was crucial precisely because it *wasn’t* another course. ATII gave me the map of what OSINT was, and KaseScenarios gave me reps. By the time I sat down for the Uncharted fundamentals course a couple of months later, I wasn’t just absorbing theory — I was connecting it to things I’d already tried, failed at, and figured out myself.

If you’re early in your OSINT journey and looking for a way to turn knowledge into skill, scenario-based challenges like these are one of the best investments you can make in yourself. Start with Sinister Obsession — and don’t peek at the answers.
