Back in 2015-2016 my world was rocked. And I don’t say that lightly. My entire reality changed and it felt like I woke up from a coma to the harsh reality that is human trafficking. I remember watching a documentary from a nonprofit organization called Fight The New Drug. What the doc outlined is the ties that pornography has with human trafficking. Now by definition, human trafficking, also known as trafficking in persons, is a crime that involves compelling or coercing a person to provide labor or services, or to engage in commercial sex acts. The coercion can be subtle or overt, physical or psychological. Exploitation of a minor for commercial sex is human trafficking, regardless of whether any form of force, fraud, or coercion was used.[^1]

So I felt a deep sorrow in my heart after watching the doc. This sorrow ignited a fire inside of me that would fuel me to study, research, volunteer and do everything I can to help bring awareness to human trafficking and the dangers of pornography. ## Brain, Heart, World (Fight The New Drug)

> **Brain, Heart, World** is a three-part documentary series produced by the non-profit organization **Fight The New Drug**. The series presents a research-driven, survivor-centered argument against pornography, framing it not as a moral issue, but as a public health and human rights crisis.
>
> Through expert interviews, brain scan analyses, and survivor testimonies, the docuseries breaks down the impact of pornography across three core dimensions:
>
> **1. The Brain (Neurological Impact)**
>
> The first part focuses on how pornography affects individual brain chemistry and mental health. Neuroscientists and psychologists explain how high-dopamine digital stimulation rewires the brain’s reward pathways, leading to desensitization, compulsion, and behavioral addiction. The episode highlights how long-term consumption often drives users to seek increasingly extreme material to achieve the same dopamine response, escalating from basic content to harmful or abusive imagery.
>
> **2. The Heart (Relational & Emotional Impact)**
>
> The second part examines how pornography alters human connection and intimacy. Therapists and former consumers discuss how regular porn exposure sets unrealistic standards for real-life relationships, fuels emotional detachment, and erodes trust between partners. It highlights how pornography often trains the brain to view human beings as sexual objects rather than whole individuals, leading to higher rates of relationship dissatisfaction, isolation, and sexual dysfunction in young adults.
>
> **3. The World (Societal & Human Rights Impact)**
>
> The final part addresses the macro-level impact of the adult industry, specifically exposing its ties to human trafficking, coercion, and sexual violence. Industry insiders and human trafficking survivors detail how major tube sites profit from unverified, non-consensual content, making it easy for sex traffickers to upload footage of victims for revenue. The episode demonstrates that the demand created by pornography directly fuels the global sex trafficking trade, confusing consent and normalizing abuse on a global scale.
