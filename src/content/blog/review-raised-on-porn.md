pubDate: 2026-07-24

Benjamin Nolot is the founder of Exodus Cry, the organization also behind the *Nefarious* documentary, so I came into *Raised on Porn* already expecting a survivor-and-research-driven perspective, and that’s exactly what it delivers. Where *Brain, Heart, World* gave me the three-part documentary framework, this book goes deeper into one piece of it: what it actually means for a generation to grow up with pornography as a primary sex educator.

Nolot’s central argument is that pornography isn’t a neutral or private habit — it actively shapes how an entire generation understands consent, intimacy, and what is considered normal in a sexual encounter. He draws a straight line from mainstream content to the demand that fuels exploitation, echoing themes I’d already encountered but with more depth and specificity.

The parts of the book that affected me most were the accounts of how early exposure — often accidental, often very young — rewires expectations before a person even has the maturity to process what they’re seeing. It’s not framed as a purity argument; it’s framed as a public health and human rights argument, which lines up with how I’d already started to think about this issue after the documentary.

This book pushed me to think less about pornography as an isolated personal choice and more as a cultural force with downstream victims — something that connects directly to why demand-side awareness matters as much as supply-side enforcement.

**Takeaway:** A sobering, research-backed look at generational impact — pairs well with *Brain, Heart, World* if you want the long-form version of “The World” segment.
