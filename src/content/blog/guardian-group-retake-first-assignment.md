I asked myself [whether I was ready](/blog/am-i-ready-guardian-group). I did not get to answer that question in my head. I got to answer it in front of Guardian Group's Volunteer and Training Assessment, again.

This time I did not walk in with my chest out. I walked in and read the assignment.

## The assignment

An escort ad. Find the true identity of the woman in it.

That is the whole task, and it is heavier than it sounds. This is not a puzzle someone invented to test me. Behind an ad like that is a person, and part of the reason this skill exists is that she may not be there by choice. So I am not going to publish her name, her handles, or anything that would let you walk the path back to her. What I can walk you through is the method, because the method is the thing worth writing down.

What I had to work with was what the ad gave me: a phone number and a couple of social handles.

## The first promising answer

I started with the phone number and ran it through FastPeopleSearch. It came back with a lead that looked good — a full name attached to that number.

Here is where I would have stopped a year ago. Full name, first search, done. Write it up and feel clever about it.

Phone numbers change hands. People switch carriers, drop numbers, inherit recycled ones, use burners. A number tells you who has been associated with it, not who is holding it today. That name was a hypothesis, not a finding, and I had no business treating it as anything else until something independent backed it up.

So I set it aside and went to work on the socials.

## Pivoting off the username

I took the username from the ad and put it into Google. That surfaced an alias account on Facebook.

And the URL did something useful. Sitting right there in the profile link was a first and middle name — not the display name on the account, the name underneath it. That is a detail worth internalizing: the vanity URL is often older and more honest than the profile it points at. People curate what they display. They forget what they typed when they signed up.

Now I had two independent paths pointing at the same person, arrived at from different directions: a name from the phone number, and a name from the username. That is better. It is still not proof.

## Making it a face

Two names that agree can still both be wrong. What I did not have yet was a face.

I ran the full name through Google and found a recent arrest record with a mugshot attached.

The mugshot was the same woman as the Facebook account. The Facebook account was the same woman as the ad.

That is the moment it stopped being an inference and became an identification. Not because a search engine told me a name, but because the ad, the social account, and the mugshot were all the same person, reached through three separate routes.

I submitted my findings, and the admin approved them.

## What the first assignment taught me

**The first plausible answer is the most dangerous one.** FastPeopleSearch handed me a name in about a minute. Had I submitted it and been right, I would have learned nothing, and I would have kept a habit that eventually gets someone hurt.

**A name is not an identity.** An identity is a name that is anchored to something — a face, a place, a timeline, something that cannot quietly be somebody else's.

**Corroborate across independent paths.** One source repeated three ways is still one source. Phone record, username pivot, and booking photo are three, and that is why the conclusion holds.

**The URL is a source.** So is the metadata, the old display name, the thing nobody thought to clean up.

This is the same discipline the [PORP exam](/blog/porp-exam-review) drilled into me and the same one that made the difference at [my first Trace Labs CTF](/blog/trace-labs-ctf-reflection) — show the chain, not just the answer. The difference between failing this assessment the first time and working through this assignment was not that I learned a new tool. FastPeopleSearch and Google are not new. What changed was that I stopped hunting for an answer and started building a case.

One assignment down. I will write up the next one as I work through it.
