On July 31, 2026, I graduated from Capella University with a Bachelor of Science in Information Technology through their FlexPath program, specializing in **Information Assurance and Cybersecurity**. It’s a milestone I want to mark here — not as a graduation announcement, but because the degree is a real part of this journey, and I want to be honest about what it actually contributed.

## Why this degree, and why FlexPath

I chose Information Assurance and Cybersecurity because everything I want to do in the anti-trafficking fight runs on a technical foundation. OSINT lives inside a bigger world of security concepts — networks, data, systems, and the people who attack and defend them. I wanted that bigger picture, not just the corner of it I’d been teaching myself.

FlexPath mattered too. The self-paced, competency-based format meant I could move fast through material I already knew from hands-on work and slow down where I needed depth. For someone stacking a degree on top of trainings, practice scenarios, and certifications, that flexibility was the difference between finishing and burning out.

## What it actually taught me

The courses matter less than the mindset, so that’s what I’ll focus on:

**Thinking in terms of risk.** Information assurance is fundamentally about asking: what could go wrong, how likely is it, and what’s the impact? That framing carries directly into OSINT work — assessing the reliability of a source, weighing how much confidence a finding deserves, and knowing what happens if I’m wrong.

**Understanding attack surfaces.** Studying how systems get compromised taught me to see digital footprints the way an adversary sees them. Every account, post, and metadata trail is part of someone’s attack surface — and that’s exactly what an OSINT investigator learns to read. Understanding the defensive side made me sharper on the research side.

**Operational security as a discipline, not an afterthought.** The degree drilled in why security controls exist and what failure costs. In OSINT — especially OSINT that touches trafficking investigations — protecting yourself, your data, and the integrity of your findings isn’t optional. Sloppy opsec can compromise more than just your own privacy.

**Rigor and documentation.** Information assurance work is documentation-heavy for a reason: findings have to hold up to scrutiny. That habit of documenting methodology and evidence carefully is the same discipline that separates useful OSINT reporting from screenshots in a folder.

## Where this fits in the journey

This degree wasn’t the beginning of my path and it isn’t the destination. It ran alongside everything else I’ve written about here — the [trainings](/blog/atii-follow-osint-data-review), the [practice scenarios](/blog/kasescenarios-sinister-obsession-review), the [courses](/blog/osint-fundamentals-uncharted-review), the [certifications](/blog/porp-exam-review), and the [humbling moments](/blog/am-i-ready-guardian-group). What it adds is a foundation underneath all of it: a formal grounding in the security principles that make OSINT work safer, sharper, and more credible.

The mission hasn’t changed. Serve the men, women, and children caught in the snares of human trafficking by becoming genuinely equipped to help. This is one more piece of that equipment.

On to the next thing.
