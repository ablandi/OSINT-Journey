pubDate: 2026-07-27

I ended up reading two books by Linda Smith, and *Invading the Darkness* felt like the natural follow-up to *Renting Lacey*. Where *Renting Lacey* focused on domestic minor sex trafficking here in the U.S., this book widens the lens to Smith’s work confronting trafficking internationally through Shared Hope International.

What stood out to me is how consistent the underlying mechanics of exploitation are across borders. Poverty, displacement, and weak legal protections get exploited by traffickers the same way vulnerability gets exploited domestically — different geography, same predatory logic. Smith’s on-the-ground accounts of rescue and aftercare work drove home how much sustained, unglamorous effort goes into actually helping a survivor rebuild a life, long after the “rescue” moment that would make a headline.

This book also reinforced something I keep coming back to in this reading list: trafficking is a global, interconnected system, not a series of isolated local incidents. The demand created in one country can directly drive exploitation in another, which is part of why awareness and demand-reduction work — the kind sparked for me by *Brain, Heart, World* — matters everywhere, not just close to home.

Finishing this book felt like closing a loop that started with *Renting Lacey*: from a single trafficked girl in America to a global system of exploitation and the people working, often invisibly, to dismantle it piece by piece.

**Takeaway:** A fitting bookend to this reading list — if you want the international, systems-level view to pair with the domestic, personal-level books above, start here.
