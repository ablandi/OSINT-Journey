I recently sat for the PORP exam — Practical OSINT Research Professional — from TCM Security, and I’ve been meaning to write down my thoughts while they’re still fresh. Short version: it was genuinely fun, harder than I expected, and it left me with sharper skills than I walked in with.

## What the exam actually felt like

This isn’t a multiple-choice knowledge test. PORP throws you into realistic, scenario-based research problems and expects you to actually dig — pulling threads across usernames, metadata, social platforms, and public records until they connect into something useful. That’s the part that made it fun. Instead of memorizing definitions, I was doing the thing I’d been practicing on my own for months: starting from a small piece of information and working outward until a picture forms.

There’s a particular kind of satisfaction in OSINT work when a dead-end lead suddenly opens back up because you thought to pivot on a detail you’d almost ignored. The exam had several of those moments, and each one felt like a small win.

## What made it challenging

The time pressure was real. Good OSINT research is naturally slow and iterative — you follow a lead, it fizzles, you back up and try a different angle. Doing that under a countdown forced me to get more disciplined about prioritizing which leads were actually worth chasing versus which ones just looked promising.

The exam also demanded a level of documentation rigor I hadn’t fully internalized before. It’s not enough to *find* the answer; you have to be able to show your work — the sources, the reasoning, the chain that got you there. That’s a habit I’ve been trying to build since I started this blog, and PORP made it non-negotiable.

## Skills it sharpened

- **Source verification** — cross-checking claims across multiple independent sources before trusting them.
- **Pivoting** — using one data point (a username, an image, a timestamp) as a bridge to the next lead instead of treating each piece of information in isolation.
- **Time management under research pressure** — knowing when to go deeper on a lead versus when to cut losses and move on.
- **Documentation discipline** — recording sourcing and reasoning clearly enough that someone else could follow the same trail.

## Why it mattered to me

Everything on this blog traces back to wanting to actually be useful in the fight against exploitation and trafficking, not just aware of it. Certifications like PORP are a concrete way to measure whether the skills I’ve been building on my own actually hold up under pressure. This one did — and it showed me exactly where I still have room to grow.

If you’re on a similar path, learning OSINT with intent rather than just curiosity, I’d recommend it. Just go in ready to work.
